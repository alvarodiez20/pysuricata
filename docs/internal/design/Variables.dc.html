Turn 4 · variables

Twelve columns, one phone

Today each column is a card about 600px tall holding two 240px stat tables and a histogram in a three-column row that collapses to one column under 1024px. Twelve columns is roughly 7,000px of scrolling, and the phone gets the worst of it. Three ways out: 4a a scannable ledger, 4b the card kept but restacked, 4c small multiples. All numbers below are the real Titanic output.

4a

Ledger — every column on one screen, expand in place

One row per column: name, type, missing, distinct, and a 28px shape of the distribution. Tap a row and it opens into the full card exactly where it sits. High-cardinality columns say so in words instead of drawing a meaningless bar.

PRO

All twelve columns are visible at once on a phone instead of one, and the section drops from roughly 7,000px to about 700px. Scan for the problem, then open only that column — which is how EDA actually goes. Identical behaviour at 390px and 1240px, so there is one layout to maintain, not two.

CON

Distributions are no longer there for free — you have to open a column to read one with axes. If your habit is scrolling past every histogram to get a feel for the data, this takes that away.

PRO

Smallest change of the three, and nothing is hidden. The histogram gains about 550px of width, so 50 bins are finally legible and the log toggle is worth using. Four ruled stat columns read better than two cramped tables.

CON

Still around 560px per column on a phone, so twelve columns is a 6,700px scroll — the core mobile problem survives. And you cannot compare two columns without scrolling between them.

4c

Small multiples, then one detail

Every column as a tile the same size, four across on desktop and two on a phone. Shape only — no axes, because at this size axes are noise. Selecting a tile opens its full detail below the grid.

PRO

Every distribution at once, at one size, so odd shapes jump out — the flat PassengerId ramp and the collapsed Fare tail are visible without reading a number. Scales to a hundred columns, where both other options break down.

CON

The most Python work, and the biggest departure from what your users know. Tiles have no axes, so they show shape and nothing else. The detail panel is real UI state — a selected column — which is more JavaScript than today's CSS-only toggles.
