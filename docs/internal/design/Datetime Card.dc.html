PRO

Every number on the face is now either identity (what and how much) or something a reader can judge. The two ratios get the baseline that makes them readable, so 27.0% stops looking like a finding — the same fix as putting 5.99 beside Jarque–Bera, and the baselines are constants, not new statistics. The timezone finally comes from source_timezone, which the accumulator has been populating and the card has been ignoring.

CON

The lede is prose on a card face, so a frame with thirty datetime columns gains thirty paragraphs — and on the irregular branch the sentence is two lines, not one. The baseline panel also costs 300px of width that the chart could have used, which conflicts with the 820px cap in phase 5d.2; on a narrow viewport it has to move below the chart.

The chart
14b

A line draws values that are not there

The timeline is a <polyline> through bucket centres. Between "84 records on 8 Jan" and "83 on 9 Jan" it draws a continuous slope, asserting intermediate values that no bucket contains — and the card's own temporal panes draw the same kind of data as bars, so one card uses two encodings for one quantity.

Today · polyline, 60 buckets the slope between two days is drawn, not measured
Proposed · bars, same data one bar per bucket · 9.2px each at 560px, 1px separators
Switch at Bars while a bucket is at least 4px, which at the 820px cap is about 180 buckets — the renderer's own ceiling. Above that a line is the honest choice, because bars would be sub-pixel and the shape is all that survives anyway.
PRO

A bucket count is a quantity per interval, which is what a bar means and not what a line means. It also matches the numeric histogram and the temporal panes, so the whole report has one encoding for counts, and the existing hotspot rects become the bars rather than sitting invisibly on top of them.

CON

A line reads a trend better, and trend is often what a datetime column is for — on 180 near-equal buckets the bars are a grey slab where the line still shows drift. Two encodings behind one threshold is also a branch to maintain and a test to write.

14c

The missing pane, and the legend on every card

The spectrum block carries a three-item legend, a title-case heading, a severity indicator and the line Hover over segments to see chunk details — an instruction that is untrue on a phone and gone in a PDF. All of it repeats on every card that has any missing value at all.

PRO

The pane leads with the finding a chunk strip exists to reveal — gaps clustering late in the read is a pipeline problem — and states the spread as a number instead of asking the reader to compare six shades. Deleting the per-card legend removes it once per affected column, which on a wide frame is most of the block's markup.

CON

A legend that lives in another section is a legend a reader may never have seen, so the colours have to be guessable from the words alone. The "last two chunks hold 71%" sentence also needs a rule for when a concentration is worth calling out, and nobody has picked one.

Order I would build these in

The timezone first, on its own, because it is a correctness bug rather than a design change and the field is already there — one line in _left_stats plus the suffix in _format_timestamp. Then the baselines in 14a, which turn two decorative percentages into two readable ones for the cost of two constants.

Then the rest of 14a — promoting the sentence and moving five statistics into the pane. 14c next, since it is mostly deletion. 14b last: it is the one genuine trade in this turn, and I would rather you looked at the two charts and said which reading matters more for the columns you profile.
