CON

Five names out of 47 is a sample presented in the place a summary belongs. It cannot tell you whether the other 42 are all at 40% or all at 0.3% — which is the difference between a broken pipeline and a few optional fields.

PRO

Answers the question the summary is for: concentrated or diffuse. Three rows characterise 47 columns as well as they characterise 470, so the block never grows. The bands are the severity classes the renderer already assigns, just counted instead of coloured.

CON

Two layouts for one block, and the switch point is a judgement — at nine affected columns a reader loses six names they would have had at eight. Mid-severity columns become a count with no identity until you scroll to the section.

What I'd do

11b, with the switch at eight. Titanic and most everyday frames stay on the list and nothing changes for them; a 200-column warehouse table gets a block that actually summarises. Ship 11a's footer first regardless — silent truncation is the defect, and the count is already sitting in total_significant unused.

One I got wrong — since corrected

An earlier draft of my tokens.css set the severity break at 50%. Your renderer uses 20% and prints that split in a legend the reader can see, so the report was self-consistent and my token file was the outlier. tokens.css is now aligned to 20 — no action needed.
