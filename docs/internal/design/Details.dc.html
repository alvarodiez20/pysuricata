Order I would build these in

10a and 10d first — both are pure rendering changes over data you already have, and both remove something actively misleading: a normalization table that contradicts its own flags, and a year chart whose only reading is "all of it". Then 10b, which needs the reservoir to expose its sample and is the single biggest gain per line of code in this document.

Then 10c, taking the length and extremes half and leaving the value sample until you have decided whether raw values belong in a shared file. 10e when you next touch datetime.py — a max and a median from the interval array are two lines. 10f last, or never, unless someone asks for drift.

What I'd do

9a for the statistics pane. It is the one change that turns the details section from an overflow area into something worth opening, and the percentile drop rule is the only fiddly part. Take 9b instead if you would rather not maintain a second chart per numeric column — it is a real improvement on its own and about a fifth of the work.

Then 9c, 9d and 9e, which I would not treat as alternatives — they fix three separate defects. 9c is where the most emoji and the most empty space are.

Two bugs to fix regardless, both visible in Embarked: Label length (avg) prints NaN and Length p90 prints an em dash, for a column whose three labels are all one character long. And the categorical Top-N control renders a single button reading 3 — a chooser with one choice; hide it when the level count is below the smallest step.
